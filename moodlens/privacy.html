Privacy

MoodLens · last updated 23 August 2026

MoodLens makes no network request at all. What you feel and what you write about it are stored on your own phone and never leave it. There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no crash reporter, no advertising network, and no third-party SDK that could add one.

What the app stores, and where

Two kinds of thing, both in the app's own private storage on your device:

  • Your entries, as two plain JSON files — one for mood check-ins (the mood you picked, any tags, the note, the date) and one for journal entries.
  • Your settings, as a handful of small values: light or dark theme, whether you have seen the introduction, and whether premium is unlocked.

If you export your entries, the CSV file is also written into that same private storage, and the app then hands it to you through the iOS share sheet. Deleting the app removes all of it.

What leaves the device

Nothing, on the app's own initiative. There is no server to reach and no code that reaches for one. The only ways anything moves are ones you start:

  • Export. You choose where the CSV goes.
  • Share MoodLens. This shares one sentence about the app — a recommendation, never an entry.
  • Send feedback. Opens your mail app with an empty message addressed to me. Nothing is attached; whatever you decide to type is what I see.

Your mood entries are not read by anything

The patterns on the Insights tab — your best and worst weekday, your most common mood, your tag counts, your streak — are computed on your phone from your own entries, by ordinary arithmetic in the app. Nothing is sent away to be analysed, and no machine learning service is involved. An earlier build described itself as an "AI mood journal"; it never was one, and the wording has been corrected rather than left standing.

In-app purchases

The app contains Apple's own in-app purchase framework, and it is currently switched off: there are no products for sale, every feature is unlocked for everybody, and the framework makes no request. If premium is ever offered, purchases go through Apple and nobody else — deliberately not through a third-party subscription service, which would mean sending purchase and device data to a company that has no business seeing it.

Permissions

None. The app asks for no camera, no microphone, no location, no contacts, no photo library and no notifications. An earlier build declared tracking permission wording meant for advertising; there has never been an ad in this app and the declaration has been removed.

Children

There is no chat, no user-generated content from anyone else, no browser and nothing to buy. Since nothing is collected from anybody, nothing is collected from children either.

Third parties

None. No advertising network, no analytics provider, no cloud backend. The app has no server component, so there is no operator to whom your entries could be disclosed and no breach that could expose them.

Not medical care

MoodLens is a private notebook with arithmetic on top. It is not a diagnosis, not therapy, and not a substitute for professional mental health care. If you are struggling, please talk to someone who can help.

Your rights

Rights to access, correct, export or erase personal data — under the UK GDPR, the EU GDPR, or the CCPA — apply to data a company holds about you. No such data exists here. Everything the app has ever written is on your phone: the export button gives you a copy, and deleting the app deletes the original.
