Recall — support

Last updated 23 August 2026 · gurbuzer1@gmail.com

Ninety seconds, one question at a time, and you type the answer. Everything below is how it actually works, not how it is meant to feel.

The marking

I typed the right country and it said I was wrong.

That is the one thing this app is built not to do, so please tell me about it — gurbuzer1@gmail.com, with the question and exactly what you typed. There is one case where it is deliberate, below.

What counts as a right answer?

Three things, in order. Accents, capitals, punctuation and a leading "the" are ignored, so cote divoire and Côte d'Ivoire are the same answer. Then any name that country is known by counts — Turkey, USA, UK, Holland, Burma, and 936 spellings in all. Then a single-character slip is forgiven: one letter added, missing or wrong.

So why was "Gambia" wrong when the answer was Zambia?

Because Gambia is a country. A slip is only forgiven when the corrected spelling could only be one country — if what you typed is itself a real country's name, you named that country rather than mistyping this one. The same goes for Iran when the answer is Iraq, and Iceland when it is Ireland.

A small number of near-misses are refused for the same reason even though they are not real names: nigeri sits exactly one letter from both Nigeria and Niger, so there is no way to tell which was meant. That is about four in every thousand possible slips. Every other one counts.

Why is "Korea" not accepted?

Because there are two, and neither is called just "Korea" — they are South Korea and North Korea. Type either and it counts. "Congo" is the case that looks the same and is not: one of the two really is named Congo, so it is accepted for that one and not for DR Congo.

The round

What does Pass do?

It sends the question to the back of the queue — it is not a skip. If the clock holds out, it comes round again, and the top of the screen tells you how many are waiting. Passing three early buys you speed now and hands you three hard ones at the end.

The timer hit zero and the game kept going.

That is deliberate. A question already on screen when the clock stops is still yours to answer; the round ends once you have finished it. The label changes to "Time — last question" so you know which one it is.

Why did I only see nine of the twenty-four questions?

Because ninety seconds is not long. The summary lists all twenty-four, including the ones you never reached, so you can see what you missed.

Can I make it easier?

Yes, on the home screen — four buttons under your record. They set how far into the country table a round reaches, and each one is labelled with what it actually contains rather than with an adjective: Top 63, Top 90, Top 125, All 179. Those counts are read off the shipped country table when the screen draws, so they cannot drift from the data.

"Top 63" means the 63 most populous countries and nothing outside them — 107 of the 297 questions. All 179 is every country in the table, and it stays the default: everyone who has played so far has played at that setting, and quietly making the game easier on an update would change what their record means.

Are the questions always the same?

No. There are 297 of them and a round deals 24, so two rounds in a row share about two questions on average. Rounds get harder as they go, and "harder" is the country's rank by population rather than a label someone chose.

The app

Does it work offline?

It only works offline, in the sense that it never goes online at all. There is no network code in it. See the privacy page.

Is there anything to buy?

No. There is no paywall, no subscription, no adverts, and nothing locked.

How do I turn off the buzz when an answer is marked?

Turn off Vibration in iOS Settings → Accessibility → Touch, or Haptics in Settings → Sounds & Haptics. The app honours both.

Can I reset my record?

Delete and reinstall the app. The record is a single small file in the app's own storage and goes with it; nothing is kept anywhere else.

A fact in a question is wrong.

Please tell me which one. Every geography fact in the app is compiled from mledoze/countries and the World Bank rather than typed by hand, so an error is either upstream or in how the question was built, and both are worth fixing. It has happened once already: the source claimed Sri Lanka borders India, which is a strait rather than a border, and that edge was dropped.
