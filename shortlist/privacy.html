Privacy

Shortlist · last updated 23 August 2026

Shortlist collects nothing, sends nothing, and has no way to. The app makes no network requests at all. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party code that could carry anything off the device.

What the app stores

One small file on your phone, and only these things go in it:

  • how many rounds you have played;
  • your best single round;
  • your totals — points scored and words solved, for the average;
  • whether you have turned haptic feedback off.

Nothing else. Not the words you were given, not the letters you asked for, not what you typed, not when you played. The file lives in the app's own storage, is never uploaded, and is deleted with the app.

What the app does not do

  • No network requests. Not on launch, not during a round, not ever.
  • No analytics, crash reporting, or attribution SDK.
  • No adverts and no ad identifier.
  • No account, sign-in, email address, or contact details.
  • No access to your contacts, photos, location, microphone, or camera.
  • No purchases. There is nothing to buy, so there is no payment data.

Why you can believe that

Because it is checked mechanically rather than promised. The repository carries a script that reads every source file the app actually ships and fails if it finds fetch, XMLHttpRequest, WebSocket, EventSource, sendBeacon, any http:// or https:// address, or a dependency whose name matches any of the common analytics, advertising or crash-reporting libraries. That script runs before a release is prepared, and the claim on this page is derived from its result rather than the other way round.

Where the words come from

The word lists are compiled into the app before it ships and are never fetched: the ENABLE word list, which decides what counts as a word, and a frequency list, which decides which words are hidden. Both are public domain. Neither is contacted at runtime; both are read once, on a build machine, into a table inside the app.

Children

Shortlist is rated 4+ and is safe for children for the simple reason that it has no way to reach anything. There is nothing to buy, nobody to talk to, and no data to collect.

Changes

If a future version ever collected anything, this page would say so before that version shipped, and the App Store privacy labels would change with it. As of this version the answer to every question on Apple's privacy questionnaire is "no data collected".
