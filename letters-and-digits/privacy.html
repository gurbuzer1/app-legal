Privacy

Letters & Digits · last updated 23 August 2026

Letters & Digits collects nothing about you. The app has no server, no account system and no network code of its own, so there is nowhere for your information to be sent even in principle. There is no analytics, no crash reporter, no advertising identifier, and no third-party SDK that could add one.

Two things reach the outside world and neither is the app doing the sending: a challenge you choose to share, and buying the full ladder. Both are described below.

Challenging a friend

When you finish a level you can tap Challenge a friend. That hands one line of text — the level number, your stars and your score — to your phone's own share sheet, and you choose where it goes. The app itself sends nothing: it writes the line and iOS does the rest. The line never contains the words you found or the numbers you played, so it cannot give the board away to the person you send it to, and the app keeps no record that you sent it.

What the app stores

Four things, on your own device, in the storage iOS gives the app for its own use. They are listed here by the exact key each one is written under:

  • landd:progress:v1 — for each level you have played, the stars you earned and the two final scores, yours and the opponent's. Which level is unlocked is worked out from those numbers rather than stored.
  • landd:settings:v1 — your four display and play preferences: theme, text size, whether the phone vibrates, and whether rounds are timed.
  • landd:answers:v1 — the last 500 answers you gave, each one a round seed, which kind of round it was, the word or number you declared, and what it scored. Nothing in the game reads this yet; it is kept so that a future version could show you how your answer compared with other players'. It has never been uploaded and the app has no ability to upload it.
  • landd:owned:v1 — a single flag recording that the one-time unlock has been bought, so a plane or a dead signal cannot take away something you paid for.

That is the entire list. None of it identifies you, none of it is transmitted anywhere, and deleting the app removes all of it. There is no backup we hold and no copy we keep.

The purchase

One non-consumable in-app purchase unlocks levels 7 to 24. It is a one-time payment, not a subscription, and there is nothing else to buy after it. Apple tells the app the localised price and whether the purchase is owned; that is everything the app learns. Your name, your email address, your payment details and your Apple Account are never visible to it. The price shown is always Apple's own string for your storefront, never a number written into the app.

Where the words come from

Every word list ships inside the app and nothing is fetched while you play: the public-domain ENABLE list decides whether a word is valid, and a public-domain frequency list decides which words the generated opponent would plausibly find. The opponent you play is worked out on your own phone from the same letters and numbers you were dealt. It is not another person, and the app says so on its first screen.

Permissions

None are requested, because none are used. The app asks for no location, no contacts, no photos, no camera, no microphone, no notifications, and no tracking permission. The only hardware it touches is the vibration motor, which needs no permission and which you can switch off on the first screen.

Children

The app is rated 4+ and is safe for children. There is no chat, no user-generated content, no browser and no advertising. Since nothing is collected from anybody, nothing is collected from children either.

Third parties

None. No advertising network, no analytics provider, no cloud backend. The app has no server component, so there is no operator to whom your data could be disclosed and no breach that could expose it.

Your rights

Rights to access, correct, export or erase personal data — under the UK GDPR, the EU GDPR, or the CCPA — apply to data a company holds about you. No such data exists here. Everything the app has ever written is on your device: deleting the app deletes all of it.

Changes

If a future version ever sends anything off the device — for example to compare your answers with other players' — this page will say so plainly, and before that version reaches the store.
