Support

Letters & Digits · last updated 23 August 2026

Anything at all — a real word the app rejected, a level that felt impossible, a purchase that will not restore — write to gurbuzer1@gmail.com. For a rejected word, the word and the level number are enough for me to find the board.

How a match works

Seven rounds against one opponent. Four rounds of letters: choose vowel or consonant one tile at a time until you have nine, then find the longest word you can make from them — a word using all nine is worth double. Two rounds of numbers: choose how many big numbers you want, then reach a three-digit target using each number at most once and staying on whole positive numbers throughout. Exactly right scores 10, within five scores 7, within ten scores 5. Finally the conundrum: nine letters, one word, ten points to whoever gets there first.

My word was real but the app rejected it

The app uses the ENABLE word list, the public-domain reference list word games are usually judged against. It is large but not infinite, and proper nouns are not in it. If a common word is genuinely missing, write in and say which one.

Who am I actually playing against?

An opponent worked out on your own device from the same letters and the same six numbers you were dealt. It is not another person and never was. Every opponent is marked CPU, and the first screen says so in as many words rather than leaving the impression to stand.

I am stuck on a level

The ladder gets harder in a measured way — each level's board was chosen by measuring how much of what was available that level's opponent actually takes, and no level is allowed to measure easier than the one before it. There is nothing to buy that makes a level easier and no way to skip one. The recap after every round shows the longest word that was sitting on the rack, or an exact route to the target, worked out by the same solver that scores you — which is the fastest way to get better.

The clock is too fast for me

Switch How you want to play to Free on the first screen. It removes the clock entirely. It is not an easier difficulty: the letters, the numbers and the opponent are identical, and only the clock is gone. There is also a Text size control on the same screen with four steps, and it enlarges the tiles as well as the words.

What do I get if I pay, and what if I do not?

Levels 1 to 6 are free and always will be. One purchase unlocks the remaining 18 permanently — no subscription and nothing else to buy afterwards. If the store cannot be reached at all, the app locks nothing rather than showing you a price you cannot pay.

I paid and the levels are still locked

Tap Restore next to the price while signed in to the Apple Account you bought with. The unlock is permanent and tied to that account, so a reinstall or a new phone brings it back. A failed store lookup never takes anything away.

How does "Challenge a friend" work?

Everyone gets the same twenty-four boards — each level pins a fixed deal, so your level 7 is the same nine letters and the same six numbers as everybody else's. After a finished level you can hand one line of text to your phone's share sheet saying the level, your stars and your score, and send it wherever you like. It deliberately does not name the words you found or the numbers you played: a challenge that listed your answers would be a cheat sheet, and the friend would beat the board without playing it. The app has no account and sends nothing itself; you choose where the line goes.

Does it work without internet?

Playing does, entirely — the dictionary ships inside the app and nothing about a match touches the network. Only buying or restoring the unlock needs a connection, because that part is Apple's.

How do I reset my progress?

Delete and reinstall the app. Nothing is stored anywhere else. A purchase is not progress: it survives a reinstall, and Restore brings it back.

Why is the game not called by the television name?

Because those names — Countdown, Des Chiffres et des Lettres, Bir Kelime Bir İşlem — are trademarks of their broadcasters. The rules of an arithmetic-and-anagram game are not, but the names are, so they appear nowhere in this app's title, listing or interface.
