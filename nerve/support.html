How to play

Fifteen rungs, four options each, two safety nets. Answer correctly and you climb; answer wrongly and you fall back to the last net you passed. Before every rung you may stop instead and keep what you have.

What the line above the options means

It is the whole game. Before each rung Nerve tells you exactly how sure you would have to be for answering to beat walking away:

You would need to be 47% sure. A blind guess is 25%.

That is arithmetic, not advice. It compares what stopping now is worth against the average of climbing and falling, and it makes no claim about what you should do.

Three rungs say 0%

Because on those three a wrong answer costs you nothing — you are standing on a safety net, or below the first one. The sentence still states the number rather than merely implying it, on every rung including the free ones, because the number is the point and it should be readable before it costs anything to misread.

Are the later rungs really harder?

Yes, and it is a fact about the data rather than a label somebody typed. Each rung draws its country from a harder slice of a list ordered by population, and the slices do not overlap: 179 countries across 15 bands of 11 or 12. The test suite walks every band to check they stay separated.

Why aren't the wrong options obviously wrong?

The classic way to ruin a hard question is to take the answer from a hard band and the wrong options from anywhere — "the capital of Kazakhstan" against Paris, Tokyo and Cairo is a reading test. Here every option on a rung comes from that rung's own band. Three thousand generated questions are checked for it before any of this ships.

What is kept between sessions

Five numbers: climbs finished, your best amount kept, the highest rung you have answered, how many climbs you walked away from, and how many reached the top. Nothing else, and nothing leaves the phone. A climb in progress is not saved — a restored ladder with an option half-chosen would throw away the pause that is the format.

Does it need a connection?

No, and it never will. Everything happens on your phone. Aeroplane mode changes nothing.

Is there anything to buy?

No. There is no in-app purchase, no subscription and no advertising. The prizes on the ladder are points and nothing else.
