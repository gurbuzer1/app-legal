How to play

One of you runs, one of you gives chase. Pick which pursuer you face, then build a pot over eight opening questions. You are offered three positions on a seven-step board: more money starts you closer to the pursuer, less money starts you further away. Get home before they catch you and you play a twelve-question final for the pot.

Every question has three answers, not four

Because a blind guess should cost something. With three options a guess is one in three, and the pursuer behind you is answering the same question.

Which offer should I take?

The game will not tell you, because that would be inventing a confidence it does not have. What it does tell you plainly is the trade: the higher offer starts you closer to the pursuer, and a harder pursuer makes every offer meaner.

Are the pursuers actually different?

Yes, and by a measured amount rather than a label. The test suite plays a weak player against all five and reports the escape rate for each; across the five they run roughly 55, 50, 45, 38 and 16 per cent. They are ordered by that measurement, so a later pursuer is never easier than an earlier one.

Where do the questions come from?

Four domains — geography, calendar, numbers and language — generated on your phone from data that ships inside the download: country facts from the mledoze/countries dataset, World Bank populations, and public-domain English word lists.

The prizes

Fictional. No money changes hands and there is nothing to exchange them for.

Is there anything to buy?

Not in this version — every pursuer is playable. If a version ever offers an unlock and it does not appear after paying, Settings has a Restore purchases button; if that does not fix it, write to me.

Clearing your record

Settings can erase your record without touching your preferences, and without deleting the app.

Does it need a connection?

No. Everything happens on your phone. Aeroplane mode changes nothing.
