Privacy

FormGenie · last updated 23 August 2026

FormGenie makes no network request, so nothing you type can leave your phone. There is no account, no server, no analytics, no crash reporter and no advertising identifier. This matters more here than in most apps: the app exists to help you fill in tax and immigration forms, so the things you type into it are exactly the things you would least like to send anywhere.

What the app stores

Only the answers you type into a form, and only on this device. They are written to the storage iOS gives the app for its own use, under a single key, so you can close a half-finished W-4 or SA100 and come back to it rather than starting again.

Those answers can include sensitive details, because government forms ask for them: names, dates of birth, national insurance and social security numbers, PAN and Aadhaar numbers, addresses, income figures. That is precisely why they are never transmitted. There is no cloud backup by the app, no sync between devices, and no copy anywhere but the phone in your hand.

What ships with the app rather than being fetched

The whole catalogue — 15 official forms across the United States, the United Kingdom and India, with 115 fields explained — is compiled into the app before it ships. Opening a form, reading an explanation and saving an answer all happen with the aeroplane switch on. Nothing is looked up, so nothing about what you are reading is observable by anyone.

Permissions the app does not ask for

None. Info.plist contains no usage-description key of any kind, which is the only way an iOS app can request access to anything. No camera, no photo library, no contacts, no location, no microphone, no notifications. There is no document upload and no OCR: an earlier build announced one and never had it.

Links out of the app

Every form links to its own official government page — irs.gov for the W-4, gov.uk for the SA100, and so on. Tapping one opens your browser and leaves FormGenie. What happens after that is between you and that website, under its own privacy policy; the app has no involvement and learns nothing about it.

Third parties

None. No advertising network, no analytics provider, no cloud backend, no AI service. In particular there is no model call of any kind: every explanation in the app is written text that ships inside it. The app has no server component, so there is no operator to whom your answers could be disclosed and no breach that could expose them.

This is not legal or tax advice

FormGenie explains what fields mean in plain English. It is not a lawyer, an accountant or an immigration adviser, it does not check your answers against your circumstances, and it does not submit anything on your behalf. Where the official form has a section this guide does not walk you through, the app names that section rather than letting you finish and believe you are done. For decisions that matter, follow the official link or speak to a professional.

Deleting your data

Empty a form's fields to clear its saved answers, or delete the app, which removes everything it ever stored. There is no account to close and nothing to request from us, because nothing ever reached us.

Your rights

Rights to access, correct, export or erase personal data — under the UK GDPR, the EU GDPR, the CCPA or India's DPDP Act — apply to data a company holds about you. No such data exists here. Everything the app has ever written is on your device.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects nothing from anyone.

Changes

If a future version ever adds a backup, a sync service or anything that sends data anywhere, this page will be updated before that version ships and the change will be described here rather than buried.
