Support

Whittle · last updated 23 August 2026

Anything at all — a clue you believe is wrong, a board that looked unsolvable, a crash — write to gurbuzer1@gmail.com. If it is about a clue, the country and the sentence are enough for me to find the board.

How to play

Eight countries and four clues. Exactly one of the eight satisfies every clue. Tap a country you think is out, then tap the clue that rules it out. Both halves have to be right, and running out of strikes ends the board.

How many mistakes do I get?

Whatever you chose. The menu offers five strikes, three, two or one, and three is the default because it is what every record in the game was set at. The setting changes only what a mistake costs: the board dealt is identical either way, because a board of unfamiliar countries is not a harder puzzle, it is a different game about whether you happen to know Comoros. Your choice is remembered between sessions.

I tapped the right country and was still marked wrong

Then the clue was the wrong reason. The game says which:

That clue is true of Peru, so it is not the reason to strike it.

Peru may well be out — but not because of that sentence. Any clue that genuinely rules the country out is accepted, not one particular "intended" answer. A country that fails two clues can be struck with either.

Why can't I just name the answer?

Because then you could win without doing the reasoning. Naming the survivor of eight is a one-in-eight guess; eliminating seven, each with the right reason out of four, is not. Striking at random wins about one board in 777 — that figure is computed exactly from the board rather than estimated, and the test suite plays 100,000 clueless runs to check the two agree.

Is every clue actually needed?

Yes, and it is checked before the board is dealt rather than promised. Each clue is the sole reason that some country on your screen falls: remove it and that country survives alongside the answer. Four things are proved by exhaustion over all 179 countries — the answer is unique in the whole table, every clue is a sole executioner, every decoy is killed by a clue that is shown, and at most one clue is the demanding kind. A board that fails any of them is discarded and another is drawn.

Some clues need me to know populations and areas

At most one of the four ever does, by design. Comparisons are also never close: the generator refuses any comparison where the two quantities are within a factor of two, so a comparative clue is never a coin flip. "Larger than Ghana" is meant to be settleable; "larger than Uganda" would not be.

Where do the facts come from?

Open data compiled into the app, never typed by hand: the mledoze/countries dataset under the ODC Open Database License for names, capitals, regions, areas, land borders and currencies, and World Bank indicator SP.POP.TOTL for populations. Two upstream errors are corrected in the build — a one-sided claim that Sri Lanka borders India, and borders pointing at countries the table does not contain.

Does it need a connection?

No, and it never will. Everything happens on your phone. Aeroplane mode changes nothing.
